Who we work with

If you want to hit a golf ball, this is for you.

Golf has a reputation as a game for the privileged. We don't see it that way. Our students are people who were told, or assumed, that the course wasn't for them.

  • Amputees and people with limb difference
  • Wheelchair users, with standing carts available
  • Veterans and first responders
  • Kids and students of every ability
  • Adults returning to the game after injury
  • Anyone who has never held a club

Our mission

Meet golfers where they are.

Adapt2Golf exists to make golf accessible to anyone who wants to play, regardless of disability, ability, experience, or resources. We adapt the game to what you have and give you the chance to find out what you can do.

That means the kid in the wheelchair who has never held a club. The veteran with a prosthetic leg who assumed sport was behind him. The student who has never set foot on a course. They're all the same kind of golfer: one who adapts to play.

“If you tell us, ‘I want to try to hit a golf ball,’ our answer is simple: let’s give it a shot.”

Programs

Three ways to get on the course.

From one-on-one adaptive lessons to full school clinics and tournament days. Every program is built so people of different abilities can take part together.

Adaptive golf lessons

One-on-one and small-group instruction for golfers with physical disabilities. Adapted equipment, adapted technique, and standing golf carts that let wheelchair users swing from their feet.

School clinics

On-site clinics for elementary and middle schools, run in your gym or on your field. Small groups of 1 to 5 with Mike directly; extra instructors come in for groups of 6 or more. Works as PE, after-school, or a one-off intro day.

How it works

Your first lesson, start to finish.

  1. 1

    Reach out

    Call, text, or email Mike. Tell him about yourself, or your students, and what you'd like to try. No experience needed.

  2. 2

    We figure out how you learn

    Some people learn by watching, some by listening, some by feel. Mike works that out in the first five minutes and teaches to it, so nothing gets complicated.

  3. 3

    You hit a golf ball

    Simple, rewarding, and fun. It isn't about how far it goes. It's about the fact that you did it.

Meet Mike

A North Shore golf pro who teaches the way you learn.

Mike Samson is a golf professional specializing in adaptive instruction and youth development. His approach starts with one question: how does this person take in information? Visual, auditory, or kinesthetic. Once he knows, the lesson is built around it.

Elementary and middle school students are his favorite age to teach. They haven't picked up bad habits yet, they're moldable, and they'll play this game for the rest of their lives. He runs groups of one to five himself and brings in additional instructors for larger groups so nobody gets lost in the crowd.

  • Adaptive instruction for physical disabilities
  • Youth development and school programs
  • Program design, including tournaments and events
