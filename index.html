Who we work with

If you want to hit a golf ball, this is for you.

Golf has a reputation as a game for the privileged. We don't see it that way. Our students are people who were told, or assumed, that the course wasn't for them.

  • Amputees and people with limb difference
  • Wheelchair users, with standing carts available
  • Veterans and first responders
  • Kids and students of every ability
  • Adults returning to the game after injury
  • Anyone who has never held a club

Our mission

Meet golfers where they are.

Adapt2Golf exists to make golf accessible to anyone who wants to play, regardless of disability, ability, experience, or resources. We adapt the game to what you have and give you the chance to find out what you can do.

That means the kid in the wheelchair who has never held a club. The veteran with a prosthetic leg who assumed sport was behind him. The student who has never set foot on a course. They're all the same kind of golfer: one who adapts to play.

“If you tell us, ‘I want to try to hit a golf ball,’ our answer is simple: let’s give it a shot.”

Meet Mike

A North Shore golf pro who teaches the way you learn.

Mike Samson is a golf professional specializing in adaptive instruction and youth development. His approach starts with one question: how does this person take in information? Visual, auditory, or kinesthetic. Once he knows, the lesson is built around it.

Elementary and middle school students are his favorite age to teach. They haven't picked up bad habits yet, they're moldable, and they'll play this game for the rest of their lives. Questions

Things people ask before their first lesson.

Do I need my own clubs?

No. Bring them if you have them, but Mike has clubs for every age, height, and grip, including adapted clubs. For school clinics, all equipment comes with us.

I use a wheelchair. Can I actually play?

Yes. Standing golf carts let wheelchair users swing from a standing position, and seated swings work too. Tell us about your mobility when you book and we'll have the right setup ready.

I've never held a golf club. Is that a problem?

Most of our students start exactly there. The first lesson is about hitting a ball and enjoying it, not about technique. No experience, no fitness level, and no golf clothes required.

Where do lessons happen?

At courses and driving ranges on Chicago's North Shore. School clinics run at your school, in the gym or on a field, so students don't need transportation.

How long is a lesson, and how much does it cost?

Private lessons usually run about an hour. Pricing depends on the format (private, small group, school clinic, or event), so send a booking request and Mike will reply with options and prices. Nobody is turned away over cost; ask about sponsored lessons.

What ages do you work with?

Everyone from kindergarten through retirees. Elementary and middle school students are a specialty, and adults returning to the game after an injury or a long break are always welcome.

What happens if it rains?

Light rain is golf weather. Heavy rain, lightning, or extreme cold means we reschedule at no charge. In winter, ask about indoor options.

Can a parent, aide, or caregiver stay for the lesson?

Absolutely. Family members, aides, and caregivers are welcome to watch or join in. For school clinics, teachers and aides stay with their students throughout.

How big are the groups?

Groups of 1 to 5 work directly with Mike. For 6 to 10 or more, additional instructors join so every student gets individual attention.

How do I book?

Use the booking form, or call or text (847) 708-7833. Mike replies within a day or two to set a time.
