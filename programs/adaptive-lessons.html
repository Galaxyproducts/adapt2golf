Adaptive golf lessons

Lessons built around your body, your equipment, and the way you learn. Whatever you bring to the tee, we adapt the game to you.

Who these lessons are for

Adaptive lessons are for anyone whose body doesn't fit the standard golf swing in the textbook. That includes amputees and people with limb difference, wheelchair users, people with limited mobility after a stroke or injury, veterans with service-related disabilities, and adults coming back to golf after a long gap. If you want to hit a golf ball, this is for you.

What a lesson looks like

The first few minutes are a conversation. Mike wants to know how you move, what feels comfortable, and how you take in information, whether that's watching, listening, or feeling the motion. Then you hit balls. The technique is adapted to you on the spot: a one-armed swing, a seated swing, a shorter club, an oversized grip, a different stance. Nothing is complicated and nothing is rushed.

Lessons run about an hour and happen at driving ranges and courses on the North Shore. One-on-one is the most common format, and small groups of up to five work well for families, friends, or support groups who want to learn together.

Equipment we provide

  • Adapted clubs: shorter shafts, lighter heads, oversized grips, and single-hand options
  • Standing golf carts that let wheelchair users swing from an upright position
  • Gripping aids and prosthetic-friendly setups
  • Standard clubs for every height, if you'd rather start there

Bring a caregiver, bring a friend

Family members, aides, and caregivers are welcome to stay for the lesson or take part. Many students find a lesson works best as a shared activity, and it helps Mike learn what support you like to have around.
